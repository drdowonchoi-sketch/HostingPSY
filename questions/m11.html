In Texas schools, What requires an ARD committee meeting?

Answer key

The ARD committee meets to make the major decisions about a student's special education program: the initial eligibility determination, IEP, and placement; reviewing the IEP at least once a year; and conducting a manifestation determination after a disciplinary change of placement. A parent may request an ARD committee meeting, and the school must either hold it or give written notice within five school days explaining why it refuses. Not every change to the IEP requires an ARD committee meeting: after the annual meeting, the parent and the school may agree in writing to amend or modify the IEP without convening a meeting. Changes to eligibility, changes in placement, and manifestation determinations, however, must be made in an ARD committee meeting.

Establishing the committee and its core functions

  • Each district must establish an ARD committee for each eligible student with a disability and for each student for whom a full individual and initial evaluation (FIIE) is conducted under §89.1011 [1].
  • The ARD committee is the IEP team defined in federal law, including 34 CFR §300.321 [1].
  • The district is responsible for the ARD/IEP team functions, including [1]:
  • IEP development (34 CFR §§300.320-300.325 and TEC §29.005)
  • placement of eligible students in private schools by the district (34 CFR §§300.145-300.147)
  • service plans for parentally placed private school students (34 CFR §§300.132, 300.138, 300.139)
  • disciplinary placement of students with disabilities (34 CFR §§300.530-300.531 and TEC §37.004)
  • evaluations, re-evaluations, and eligibility determinations (34 CFR §§300.302-300.306)
  • least restrictive environment (34 CFR §§300.114-300.117)
  • The excerpt of §89.1050 also lists several TEC provisions (§§28.006, 28.0211, 28.0212, 28.0213) but is cut off before the list ends [1].

Specific triggers

1. Before enrollment in special education. The district must establish a committee to develop the child's IEP before the child is enrolled in a special education program [3]. An IEP is a written statement "developed, reviewed, and revised in a meeting" under 34 CFR §§300.320-300.324 [14].

2. After an initial evaluation. The ARD committee must decide initial eligibility and, if appropriate, the IEP and placement within 30 calendar days of completing the written FIIE report [19].

  • If the 30th day falls in the summer when school is not in session, the committee must meet by the 15th school day of the following school year [19].
  • If the FIIE report indicates the student needs extended school year services that summer, the committee must meet as expeditiously as possible after the report is completed [19].

3. Annual review. The IEP team must review the IEP periodically, and not less than annually, to determine whether annual goals are being achieved. It must revise the IEP as appropriate, for example to address any lack of expected progress [9]. Texas rules likewise require the ARD committee to review certain IEP issues at least annually [6].

4. Nonpublic residential placement. Before the ARD committee places or refers a student to a nonpublic residential program, it must initiate and conduct a meeting to develop an IEP under 34 CFR §§300.320-300.325 [7].

5. Disagreement. When mutual agreement on all required IEP elements is not reached, the disagreeing parent must be offered a single opportunity to recess and reconvene [6].

  • The reconvened meeting must occur within ten school days unless the parties agree otherwise [6].
  • The recess opportunity is not required if the student's presence on campus presents a danger of physical harm, or if the student has committed an expellable offense or an offense that may lead to a disciplinary alternative education program placement [6].

6. Transition. The student must be invited when a purpose of the meeting is consideration of postsecondary goals and transition services. Parent notice must say so beginning with the first IEP in effect when the child turns 16, or younger if the IEP team determines it is appropriate [5][10].

Situations where a full meeting is not necessarily required

  • After the annual meeting, the parent and district may agree not to convene an IEP team meeting to make changes, and may instead develop a written amendment. The IEP team must still be informed of the changes [9].
  • Required members may be excused from attending if the 34 CFR §300.321(e) conditions are met, including written parent and district agreement [11].
  • If a parent revokes consent in writing for continued special education services, the district is not required to convene an IEP team meeting or develop an IEP for further provision of services [4].

In Texas public schools, an ARD (Admission, Review, and Dismissal) committee meeting is required when a decision or review is needed regarding a student’s eligibility for special education, Individualized Education Program (IEP), services, placement, or other matters governed by special education law.

Texas follows the federal Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) and state rules established by the Texas Education Agency (TEA).

What requires an ARD committee meeting?

The following are the most common situations.

Initial eligibility for special education: After the initial evaluation, the ARD committee determines whether the student qualifies for special education and, if eligible, develops the initial IEP. The required parent consent must be obtained before initial services begin.

Annual review of the IEP: The committee must review the IEP at least once every 12 months and revise it as appropriate.

Reevaluation: When a student needs reevaluation to determine continued eligibility or educational needs, the ARD committee reviews the evaluation information and makes the necessary decisions. Reevaluations generally occur at least once every three years unless the parent and school agree they are unnecessary, and may occur more frequently when warranted.

Changes to placement: A proposed change in a student's educational placement, including a change involving the student's instructional setting or special education program, may require an ARD meeting.

Changes to special education services or the IEP: Significant decisions involving goals, accommodations, modifications, related services, or specially designed instruction may require committee consideration.

Disciplinary matters involving a student with a disability: Certain disciplinary removals, particularly a change of placement resulting from discipline, require ARD committee action, including a manifestation determination review (MDR) when required by law.

Transition planning: Beginning no later than the first IEP in effect when the student turns 14 in Texas, the ARD committee must address the required transition-related planning and services.

Graduation or dismissal from special education: The committee addresses applicable graduation requirements and decisions about ending special education eligibility or services.

Parent request for an ARD meeting: A parent may request a meeting to discuss their child's IEP or special education needs. The school must respond in accordance with applicable Texas rules; a request does not automatically mean every requested meeting must be convened.

  • Behavior intervention or safety concerns: A review may be needed to develop or revise a behavior intervention plan (BIP), particularly when behavior affects learning, safety, or the student's educational program.

Texas regulations specifically assign these responsibilities to the ARD committee.

Initial evaluation / eligibility. In Texas schools, an ARD committee meeting is required for a number of reasons, including:

Initial evaluation

After a student's initial evaluation report is complete, an ARD committee must meet to determine if the student is eligible for special education and related services. The committee must make their decision within 30 calendar days of the report's completion.

IEP development, review, or revision

The ARD committee meets at least once a year to develop, review, or revise a student's Individualized Education Program (IEP).

Student transfer

When a student transfers to a new school district, the new district must have an ARD meeting within 30 calendar days of the evaluation report's completion.

Emergent bilingual or English learner

When a student is identified as an emergent bilingual or English learner, the ARD committee must work with the language proficiency assessment committee to determine appropriate identification and reclassification procedures.

Expulsion or placement in JJAEP

When a child with a disability is being considered for expulsion or placement in the Juvenile Justice Alternative Education Program, the ARD committee must invite the JJAEP administrator.

The ARD committee is also known as the individualized education program (IEP) team in other states. The committee typically includes the parents or adult student, a general education teacher, a special education teacher, a campus administrator, and a diagnostician or school psychologist.
