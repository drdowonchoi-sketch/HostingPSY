In Texas schools, What does FAPE require in school settings?

Answer key

If a child qualifies for special education and related services, the school must provide a free appropriate public education (FAPE) in the least restrictive environment. FAPE is delivered through the IEP: the ARD committee develops the IEP and the school implements it. Before providing special education services for the first time, the school must obtain the parent's informed written consent and make reasonable efforts to get it. If the parent refuses or does not respond, the school may not use mediation or due process to override the refusal, no services are provided, and the school is not in violation of its duty to make FAPE available.

Facts an answer must state: least restrictive environment · IEP · consent  ·  Governing law: IDEA §300.300

Short answer: The context does not include the IDEA's regulatory definition of FAPE (34 CFR §300.17). It does set out what FAPE requires under Section 504, what IDEA requires for the IEP and service delivery, and how Texas rules apply FAPE in different placements.

1. Section 504 (34 CFR Part 104)

  • Who is covered: A recipient operating a public elementary or secondary program must provide FAPE to each qualified person with a disability in its jurisdiction, regardless of the nature or severity of the disability [1].
  • "Appropriate" education: This means regular or special education and related aids and services that:
  • are designed to meet individual educational needs as adequately as the needs of nondisabled students are met, and
  • are based on adherence to the procedures in §§104.34, 104.35, and 104.36 [1].
  • IEP as one means of compliance: Implementing an IEP developed under the Education of the Handicapped Act is one way to meet the "as adequately as" standard [1].
  • "Free" education: Services must be provided without cost to the student or parents, except for fees imposed on nondisabled students or their parents. If the recipient places or refers a student to outside services, it must pay the costs and remains responsible for meeting the requirements. Funds from other public or private agencies may be used, and insurers are not relieved of valid obligations [1].
  • Educational setting (§104.34):
  • Students must be educated with nondisabled peers to the maximum extent appropriate.
  • The regular environment is the default unless the recipient demonstrates that satisfactory education there, with supplementary aids and services, cannot be achieved.
  • Any alternative setting must take into account its proximity to the student's home.
  • Students must also participate with nondisabled peers in nonacademic and extracurricular activities, including meals and recess, to the maximum extent appropriate.
  • Separate facilities for students with disabilities must be comparable to the recipient's other facilities, services, and activities [13].

2. IDEA (34 CFR Part 300)

  • Child find (§300.111): The state must ensure that all children with disabilities who need special education and related services are identified, located, and evaluated. This includes homeless children, wards of the State, and children in private schools, regardless of severity. It also includes children who are advancing from grade to grade and highly mobile children, including migrant children [14].
  • Eligibility (§300.8): A "child with a disability" has one of the listed impairments, as determined by an evaluation under §§300.304–300.311, and by reason of it needs special education and related services. A child who has one of those impairments but needs only a related service, not special education, is not a child with a disability under IDEA, unless the related service is considered special education under State standards [18].
  • IEP content (§300.320): The IEP must include:
  • present levels of academic achievement and functional performance;
  • measurable annual goals;
  • how progress will be measured and reported;
  • the special education, related services, and supplementary aids and services to be provided, based on peer-reviewed research to the extent practicable, plus program modifications or supports for school personnel;
  • an explanation of any nonparticipation with nondisabled children;
  • assessment accommodations or alternate assessment justification;
  • the projected start date, frequency, location, and duration of services [15][16].
  • Transition and age of majority: Beginning no later than the first IEP in effect when the child turns 16 (or younger if the IEP Team decides), the IEP must include postsecondary goals and transition services. It must also include a statement that the child has been informed of rights that will transfer at the age of majority, beginning no later than one year before that age [17].
  • Implementation (§300.323):
  • An IEP meeting must be held within 30 days of determining that the child needs special education and related services.
  • Services must be made available as soon as possible after the IEP is developed.
  • The IEP must be accessible to each teacher and provider responsible for implementing it, and each must be informed of their specific responsibilities and the required accommodations, modifications, and supports [2].
  • For in-state transfers, the new agency, in consultation with parents, must provide FAPE, including services comparable to those in the prior IEP, until it adopts that IEP or develops a new one meeting §§300.320–300.324 [2].
  • The excerpt on out-of-state transfers is cut off, so its full text is not available [2].
  • Physical education (§300.108): Physical education, specially designed if necessary, must be available to every child with a disability receiving FAPE, subject to the stated exception. Children must have the opportunity to participate in regular physical education unless they are enrolled full time in a separate facility or need specially designed physical education under the IEP [7].
  • Interagency obligations (§300.154): A noneducational public agency obligated to provide or pay for services necessary for FAPE must fulfill that obligation. If it fails to do so, the LEA (or the State agency responsible for developing the IEP) must provide or pay for the services in a timely manner and may seek reimbursement [8].

3. Texas-specific rules

  • Scope (19 TAC §89.1001): Special education and related services must be provided to eligible students in accordance with federal law and regulations, state statutes, and State Board of Education, SBEC, and commissioner rules, including IDEA. TJJD, TSBVI, TSD, and TDCJ education programs must comply and are monitored by TEA [9].
  • Residential facilities: A district must initiate Child Find outreach for any residential facility within its boundaries and provide required services to eligible students. The exception is where the facility demonstrates that another educational program provider is serving them. The district must contact the facility at least twice per year to conduct Child Find and offer services [9].
  • Nonpublic placements:
  • Day placements: The ARD committee must determine that a nonpublic or non-district operated day placement is required for the student to make reasonable educational progress and receive FAPE. Districts contract for these when they cannot provide the services [12][10].
  • Residential placements: The ARD committee must determine that a residential placement is necessary for FAPE, and it is not a placement intended primarily for medical care and treatment. Before placing or referring a student, the ARD committee must initiate and conduct a meeting [11].
  • TSBVI students (TEC §30.021): The district of residence is responsible for assuring FAPE for each district student placed in the school's regular school year program. It must also ensure that all legally required IEP development and review meetings are conducted [6].

4. Relationship between Section 504 and IDEA

All students eligible under IDEA are protected by Section 504 and Title II, but not all students protected by Section 504 are IDEA-eligible [4]. A district that finds a student ineligible under IDEA must still consider whether the student has a disability under Section 504 or Title II [3]. A student with a disability who requires only a related service may be entitled to FAPE services under Section 504 [4].

Short answer: In Texas, FAPE means special education and related services that are provided at public expense, under public supervision and direction, and without charge. They must meet TEA standards, cover preschool, elementary, or secondary education, and be provided in conformity with the student's IEP [7]. Federal IDEA and Section 504 add further requirements, listed below.

Texas definition (19 TAC §89.1115)

Consistent with 20 USC §1401(8), FAPE means special education and related services that [7]:

  • (A) are provided at public expense, under public supervision and direction, and without charge;
  • (B) meet the standards of TEA;
  • (C) include preschool, elementary, or secondary school education; and
  • (D) are provided in conformity with the student's individualized education program (IEP).

Texas rules also require that special education and related services be provided in accordance with applicable federal law and regulations, state statutes, State Board of Education, SBEC, and commissioner rules, and IDEA [8].

IDEA (34 CFR Part 300)

  • Purpose: FAPE emphasizes special education and related services designed to meet children's unique needs and prepare them for further education, employment, and independent living [18].
  • Physical education: Physical education services, specially designed if necessary, must be available to every child with a disability receiving FAPE. The exception is where the agency enrolls nondisabled children in the same grades and does not provide physical education to them [5].
  • Private placement: An LEA need not pay for private school if it made FAPE available and the parents chose private placement [6]. A hearing officer or court may require reimbursement if the agency had not made FAPE available in a timely manner and the private placement is appropriate [6]. Disagreements are subject to due process under §§300.504–300.520 [6].

Section 504 (34 CFR Part 104)

  • General duty: Public elementary and secondary recipients must provide FAPE to each qualified person in their jurisdiction, regardless of the nature or severity of the handicap [2].
  • Appropriate education: This means regular or special education and related aids and services that are designed to meet individual needs as adequately as the needs of nonhandicapped persons are met. They must also be based on the procedures in §§104.34, 104.35, and 104.36 [2]. Implementing an IEP is one means of meeting this standard [2].
  • Free education: Services are provided without cost to the student or parents, except for fees imposed on nonhandicapped persons [2].
  • Outside placements: If the recipient places or refers a student to a program it does not operate, it remains responsible for ensuring the requirements are met [2][9].
  • Educational setting:
  • Students must be educated with nonhandicapped peers to the maximum extent appropriate [4].
  • Students must be placed in the regular environment unless the recipient demonstrates that satisfactory education there with supplementary aids and services cannot be achieved [4].
  • If placed elsewhere, the proximity of the alternate setting to the student's home must be considered [4].
  • Nonacademic and extracurricular settings carry parallel participation requirements [4][15].
  • Evaluation and placement:
  • A preplacement evaluation is required before initial placement and any significant change in placement [13].
  • Evaluation procedures must meet validity, trained-administrator, and sensory/manual/speaking-skill requirements [13].
  • Placement decisions must draw on a variety of sources, be documented, and be made by a group of knowledgeable persons in conformity with §104.34 [13].
  • Periodic reevaluation procedures are required [13][14].
  • Staffing and implementation (OCR guidance): Regular education teachers implement the aids and services they can. The district remains ultimately responsible for ensuring sufficient qualified personnel [9].
  • Remedy for denial of FAPE: Failure to evaluate a student who needed services may result in compensatory services for the period FAPE was not offered [12].

Texas residential settings

  • A district must conduct Child Find in residential facilities within its boundaries and provide services to eligible students. The exception is where the facility shows that another education provider supplies the services. The district must still contact the facility at least twice per year [8].
  • LEAs must provide or ensure FAPE for students with disabilities residing in residential facilities in their geographic area, and for students they place there for educational purposes [7].
  • A nonpublic residential placement for FAPE requires an ARD committee determination that residential placement is necessary [11].

In Texas schools, FAPE stands for Free Appropriate Public Education. It is a legal requirement under the federal Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) that eligible students with disabilities receive the special education and related services they need to access an appropriate education at no cost to their families. Texas public school districts and charter schools must follow these requirements. [Individuals with Disabilities Education Act +1]

What does FAPE require in a school setting?

1. Appropriate, individualized instruction

The school must provide instruction designed to meet the student's individual educational needs, rather than expecting the student to fit a one-size-fits-all program. The student's needs and services are documented in an Individualized Education Program (IEP), developed in Texas by the Admission, Review, and Dismissal (ARD) committee. [Texas Education Agency +1]

2. Required special education and related services

Depending on the student's needs, FAPE may include:

Specialized instruction and individualized teaching strategies.

Speech-language therapy, occupational therapy, or physical therapy when required.

Accommodations, assistive technology, and supplementary aids.

Behavioral supports and other related services necessary for the student to benefit from special education.

The school must provide the services specified in the student's IEP. [Texas Education Agency +1]

3. Education in the least restrictive environment (LRE)

Students with disabilities must be educated alongside students without disabilities to the maximum extent appropriate. Schools must consider general education classrooms with appropriate supports before moving a student to a more restrictive setting.

A separate classroom, specialized school, or other restrictive placement is appropriate only when the student cannot be satisfactorily educated in a regular classroom even with supplementary aids and services. [Individuals with Disabilities Education Act +1]

4. Services at no cost to the family

Required special education and related services must be provided at public expense, under public supervision and direction, and without charge to the family. [Individuals with Disabilities Education Act]

5. Implementation and review of the IEP

The school must implement the student's IEP and review it periodically to determine whether the services and supports remain appropriate. The ARD committee must consider the student's individual needs, educational progress, and placement.

6. Access to education even when behavior or discipline is an issue

A student with a disability who is suspended or expelled may still be entitled to educational services under IDEA. When disciplinary removals trigger additional legal requirements, the district must follow those requirements and ensure the student continues receiving the services necessary to meet applicable educational goals. [Texas Education Agency +1]

What FAPE does not mean

FAPE does not necessarily guarantee the best possible education, a particular teacher, or a specific classroom. Under the Supreme Court's Endrew F. v. Douglas County School District decision, an IEP must be reasonably calculated to enable a child to make progress appropriate in light of the child's circumstances.

What it requires in practice:

1. Services designed for the individual student. Each school district must provide special education and related services to eligible students with disabilities in order to meet the unique needs of those students in accordance with 34 CFR §§300.114-300.118 and state law (19 TAC §89.1005).

2. An IEP developed by the ARD committee. Each district must establish an admission, review, and dismissal (ARD) committee for each eligible student and for each student for whom a full individual and initial evaluation is conducted; the ARD committee is the IEP team defined in federal law, and the district is responsible for all IEP-team functions under 34 CFR §§300.320-300.325 and Texas Education Code §29.005 (19 TAC §89.1050).

3. Least restrictive environment. Placement decisions run through the LRE rules at 34 CFR §§300.114-300.117. In career and technical education, for example, a student with a disability must be instructed in accordance with the IEP in the least restrictive environment as determined by the ARD committee, and may be served in a separate program only if the student cannot receive a free appropriate public education in a regular CTE program using supplementary aids and services (19 TAC §75.1023).

4. A full instructional day. A student receiving special education and related services must have available an instructional day commensurate with that of students not receiving those services, and the day may be modified only when the ARD committee determines it necessary (19 TAC §89.1005).

5. Actual implementation by staff. Each district must ensure every teacher who instructs a student with a disability has access to relevant sections of the current IEP, is informed of their specific responsibilities for implementing it — goals, objectives, accommodations, modifications, supports — and has an opportunity to request assistance with implementation (19 TAC §89.1075). Teacher standards reinforce that all school staff have a legal responsibility to fully implement an IEP (19 TAC §235.131).

6. More restrictive placements at district expense when needed. A residential placement occurs when the ARD committee determines the student requires it to attain reasonable educational progress and to be provided FAPE; it is not a placement intended primarily for medical care, and a day placement is similarly ARD-determined when needed for reasonable educational progress and FAPE (19 TAC §89.1092, §89.1094).

7. The FAPE offer governs private-placement disputes. When a parentally placed private school student is referred to the local district, the district must convene an ARD meeting to determine whether it can offer FAPE; if it determines it can, the district is not responsible for the private placement (19 TAC §89.1096).

8. Students in residential facilities are still covered. A statewide MOU exists to establish the respective responsibilities of school districts and residential facilities for the provision of FAPE as required by IDEA for children with disabilities residing in those facilities (19 TAC §89.1115).

Documentation. Districts must maintain an eligibility folder for each student receiving special education, including referral data, notices and consents, evaluation reports and supporting data, ARD committee reports, and the IEPs with supporting data.

It applies even if the student is advancing from grade to grade and has not failed or been retained. The right to FAPE ends when a student graduates with a regular high school diploma under 19 TAC §89.1070; a certificate of attendance or coursework completion is not a regular diploma.

What it consists ofIDEA defines FAPE as special education and related services that (1) are provided at public expense; (2) meet the standards of the state education agency; (3) include an appropriate preschool, elementary, or secondary education in the state involved; and (4) are provided in conformity with an IEP meeting 34 C.F.R. §§300.320–324. In Texas, that means the special education and related services in the student's IEP that the ARD (admission, review, and dismissal) committee determines are necessary — at public expense.

School-setting requirements in Texas

Child Find: districts must identify and evaluate students suspected of having a disability, including students passing from grade to grade and students who are homeless, in state care, in private school, or highly mobile.
