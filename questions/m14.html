In Texas schools, when is an initial evaluation of a child conducted?

Answer key

An initial evaluation (the full individual and initial evaluation, or FIIE) is conducted after a referral or a request for evaluation and only with the parent's informed written consent. When a parent requests an evaluation from the director of special education or a district administrative employee, the school must, within 15 school days, either give prior written notice of its proposal to evaluate together with the procedural safeguards and an opportunity to consent, or give prior written notice of its refusal. Once the school receives consent, the evaluation must be completed and the written report provided within 45 school days, extended if the student is absent three or more school days.

Short answer: In Texas, an initial evaluation (a full individual and initial evaluation, or FIIE) is conducted before special education and related services are first provided. It requires informed parental consent, and the written report is generally due within 45 school days of the district receiving that consent.

1. Before special education services begin

  • A public agency must conduct a full and individual initial evaluation before the initial provision of special education and related services to a child with a disability [4].

2. When a referral is made

  • Referral for an FIIE must be part of the district's multi-tiered system of academic and behavioral supports [2].
  • A referral or request may be initiated at any time by school personnel, the parent or legal guardian, or another person involved in the child's education or care [2]. Federal rules likewise allow either a parent or the public agency to request an initial evaluation [4].
  • District personnel must refer a student if the student continues to struggle despite interventions and support services, or at any time they suspect a disability and a possible need for special education [2].
  • A district cannot require participation in interventions for any specific length of time before referral or an FIIE [2].
  • A parent of a child not enrolled in a school district or open-enrollment charter school may also request an FIIE [3].

3. Response to a written parent request

  • If a parent submits a written request to the director of special education or a district administrative employee (such as a principal), the district must act within 15 school days [2]. It must provide either:
  • prior written notice of its proposal to evaluate, a procedural safeguards notice, and the TEA Overview of Special Education for Parents form [2][6]; or
  • prior written notice of its refusal to evaluate, with the same Overview form and procedural safeguards notice [2][6].
  • Prior written notice must follow 34 CFR §300.503 and the procedural safeguards notice must follow 34 CFR §300.504 [2][6]. A procedural safeguards copy must be given upon initial referral or parent request for evaluation [17].
  • When the district initiates the referral, it must give the parent the same materials described for a proposal to evaluate [6].

4. Consent

  • After providing notice consistent with 34 CFR §§300.503 and 300.504, the agency must obtain informed parental consent before conducting the evaluation [5].
  • Consent to evaluate is not consent to provide special education services [5].
  • If the parent does not consent, the school may, but is not required to, pursue mediation or a due process hearing. Declining to pursue the evaluation does not violate the IDEA Child Find duty [8].

5. Timeline for completion

  • General rule: The written FIIE report must be completed within 45 school days after the district receives written parental consent. The period is extended by the number of school days the student was absent if the student was absent three or more days during the period [1][6]. The timeline begins on the first school day after the district receives consent [8].
  • Under five or private/home school: The same 45-school-day timeline applies to students under five by September 1 who are not enrolled in public school, and to students in a private or home school setting [1][6].
  • Consent received 35 to fewer than 45 school days before the last instructional day: The report must be provided to the parent by June 30, and the ARD committee must meet by the 15th school day of the following school year [1][6][8].
  • Absences or late consent: If the student is absent three or more days in that period, or consent arrives fewer than 35 school days before the last instructional day, the general 45-school-day timeline applies [1][6][8].
  • Federal baseline: The federal rule requires evaluation within 60 days of consent, or within the State's timeframe if the State sets one [4]. Texas sets the 45-school-day timeframe [1].
  • Federal exceptions: The timeframe does not apply if the parent repeatedly fails or refuses to produce the child, or if the child enrolls in another public agency before the previous agency determines eligibility [4]. For transfers, the exception applies only if the new agency is making sufficient progress and the parent and agency agree on a specific completion date [4][7]. Texas rules direct the new district to coordinate with the previous district under 34 CFR §300.301(d)(2) and (e) and §300.304(c)(5) [7].

6. After the evaluation

  • The ARD committee must decide initial eligibility and, if appropriate, the IEP and placement within 30 calendar days of completing the written FIIE report [7][8].
  • If the 30th day falls in the summer when school is not in session, the ARD committee must meet by the 15th school day of the next school year. If the report indicates the student needs extended school year services that summer, it must meet as expeditiously as possible [7].
  • The parent must receive a copy of the report as soon as possible, and no later than five school days before the initial ARD meeting (or by June 30 where applicable) [7][8].
