In Texas schools, Who can teach deaf or hard of hearing students?

Answer key

Texas requires that teachers certified in the education of students who are deaf or hard of hearing be available to students who are deaf or hard of hearing, including students with deaf-blindness, through one of the school district's instructional options, a regional day school program for the deaf, or a shared services arrangement with other school districts. Teachers assigned to instruct children from birth through age two who are deaf or hard of hearing must be certified in the education of students who are deaf and hard of hearing. In addition, when a child is suspected of being or is documented as deaf or hard of hearing, a teacher certified in deaf and hard of hearing education must be a member of the child's ARD committee.

Short answer: Texas requires that special education and related service personnel be certified, endorsed, or licensed in their area of assignment. For students who are deaf or hard of hearing, the rules specifically call for teachers certified in the education of students who are deaf or hard of hearing.

General personnel requirement

  • All special education and related service personnel must be certified, endorsed, or licensed in their area of assignment under 34 CFR §300.156; TEC §§21.002, 21.003, and 29.304; or appropriate state agency credentials [1].
  • Under 34 CFR §300.156, special education teachers must hold full state certification (or have passed the state licensing exam and hold a license) and at least a bachelor's degree. They must not have had certification requirements waived on an emergency, temporary, or provisional basis [13][14].
  • A teacher in an alternate-route certification program may be considered to meet the standard if the program's conditions are met. These include intensive supervision, a limit of three years, and satisfactory progress toward full certification [14].

Teachers certified in the education of students who are deaf or hard of hearing

  • Teachers certified in this area must be available to students who are deaf or hard of hearing, including deaf-blindness. Availability may be through the district's instructional options, a regional day school program for the deaf, or a shared services arrangement with other districts [1].
  • Teachers assigned full-time or part-time to students from birth through age two who are deaf or hard of hearing, including deaf-blindness, must be certified in education for students who are deaf and hard of hearing [9].
  • The ARD committee for a student suspected or documented to be deaf or hard of hearing must include a teacher certified in the education of students who are deaf or hard of hearing. For suspected or documented deaf-blindness, it must include both a teacher certified in visual impairments and a teacher certified in deaf/hard of hearing education [4].
  • The special education teacher or provider on the ARD committee must be appropriately certified or licensed as required by 34 CFR §300.156 [4].
  • Other special education teachers may be assigned to any level of a basic special education program for eligible students ages 3–21, within the limits of their certification, except for the listed exceptions [1].

Other personnel

  • Interpreters: Interpreting services for students who are deaf must be provided by an interpreter certified in the appropriate language mode(s), if certification is available. The interpreter must be certified by or a certified member of the Registry of Interpreters for the Deaf or the Texas Board for Evaluation of Interpreters [9].
  • Speech therapy: Persons providing speech therapy instructional services must hold a valid TEA certificate in speech and hearing therapy or speech and language therapy, or a valid state license as a speech/language pathologist [1].
  • Paraprofessionals: Educational aides must be certified and may be assigned to work with eligible students, teachers, and related service personnel [9]. Federal rules allow appropriately trained and supervised paraprofessionals to assist in providing services [13].

Service arrangements

  • Districts may meet students' needs through district specialty staff, contracts with other qualified agencies, support from regional education service centers or adjacent districts, short- or long-term services through the Texas School for the Deaf or regional day school programs, or other agency-approved arrangements [10].
  • The Texas School for the Deaf provides educational services to students who are deaf or hard of hearing [6].

1. Who is qualified to teach deaf or hard-of-hearing students?

Under the Texas Education Agency (TEA) rules, 19 Texas Administrative Code §231.709, the following certificates qualify a teacher for a dedicated Teacher of the Deaf and Hard of Hearing assignment:

The teacher must be available to serve students with hearing impairments.

2. Can a general education or special education teacher teach these students?

Yes, depending on the teaching assignment. Texas law distinguishes between general classroom instruction and a specialized assignment as a teacher of deaf or hard-of-hearing students.

General education teachers: May teach deaf or hard-of-hearing students in their regular classrooms, but must be adequately prepared to provide instruction to those students.

Special education teachers: May provide special education instruction if they hold the appropriate certification for their assignment.

Teachers of the Deaf and Hard of Hearing: Have the specialized certification required for the dedicated teaching assignment described in the state rules.

Interpreters and other support personnel: May support communication and access to instruction, but an interpreter does not automatically qualify as the classroom teacher.

Texas Education Code §29.304 requires appropriate qualified staff and specifies that general and special education personnel working with these students must be adequately prepared.
