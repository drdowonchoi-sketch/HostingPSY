In Texas schools, When does a child become an adult student?

Answer key

A student becomes an adult student at age 18, or earlier if a court has removed the student's disabilities of minority (emancipation). At that point all special-education rights except the right to receive notices transfer from the parent to the student, unless a parent or another person has been granted guardianship of the student (under federal law, unless the student has been determined incompetent). Choosing a supported decision-making agreement or another alternative to guardianship does not stop the transfer. At least one year before the student turns 18, the IEP must state that the student was told about the transfer and that the student and parents received information on guardianship, alternatives to guardianship including supported decision-making, and supports for independent living. After the transfer, the district sends required notices to both the adult student and the parent, and notifies both in writing when the transfer happens. For a student incarcerated in an adult or juvenile correctional institution, all rights, including receiving notices, transfer to the student unless a guardian has been appointed. Turning 18 does not end school: compulsory attendance runs to the 19th birthday, students are entitled to attend until age 21, and may be admitted until 26 to finish a diploma.

Special education (IDEA and Texas law)

  • A student with a disability who is 18 or older, or whose disabilities of minority have been removed under Chapter 31, Family Code, has the same right to make educational decisions as a student without a disability [1]. The district must still provide any notice required by the subchapter or 20 U.S.C. §1415 to both the student and the parents. All other parental rights under those provisions transfer to the student [1].
  • The Commissioner's rules say all IDEA Part B rights granted to the parent, other than the right to receive required notices, transfer to the student upon reaching age 18 [2]. This does not apply if the parent or another individual has been granted guardianship under the applicable guardianship law [2]. The rule cross-references 34 CFR §300.320(c), §300.520, and TEC §29.017 [2].
  • The federal rule lets a state provide for transfer of rights when a child reaches the age of majority under State law, except for a child determined to be incompetent under State law [5].
  • TEA guidance likewise says transfer occurs unless the adult student has been determined incompetent under state law and a court has appointed a legal guardian [7][8].

Under the Texas rule, this applies to an 18-year-old incarcerated student, unless the parent or another individual has been granted guardianship under Texas Estates Code, Title 3 [2].

Related procedural requirements

  • Beginning at least one year before the student turns 18, the IEP must state that the student has been informed of the rights that will transfer. It must also state that the student has been given information on guardianship, alternatives to guardianship (including a supported decision-making agreement under Texas Estates Code, Chapter 1357), and other supports for independent living [1][2][7][8]. This tracks 34 CFR §300.320(c) [2][18].
  • When the student reaches 18, the district must give separate written notice of the transfer of rights to both the adult student and the parent [2][5]. This is separate from the IEP statement requirement [2].

Education records (FERPA)

  • An "eligible student" is a student who has reached 18 or is attending a postsecondary institution [4]. When a student becomes an eligible student, the rights and consent authority of parents under FERPA transfer to the student [3].
  • FERPA does not prevent disclosure of education records to a parent without the eligible student's consent if the disclosure meets the conditions in §99.31(a)(8), (a)(10), (a)(15), or any other provision of §99.31(a) [3].

  • The school district must still provide any notice required by Subchapter A or 20 U.S.C. §1415 to both the student and the parents. All other rights accorded to parents transfer to the student [1].
  • Rights also transfer to students incarcerated in an adult or juvenile, state or local correctional institution [1]. 19 TAC §89.1049(b) applies this to an 18-year-old incarcerated student, unless a parent or other individual has been granted guardianship [9].

Federal framework

  • 34 CFR §300.520 permits a state to transfer rights when a child with a disability reaches the age of majority under state law, except for a child determined to be incompetent under state law [7]. Texas implements this through TEC §29.017 and 19 TAC §89.1049 [9].
  • Under FERPA, an "eligible student" is one who has reached 18 or is attending a postsecondary institution. When a student becomes eligible, FERPA rights and consent requirements transfer from the parents to the student [5][2].

Exception: guardianship. Rights do not transfer if the student's parent or another individual has been granted guardianship [9]. TEA guidance likewise says transfer occurs unless the student has been determined incompetent under state law and a court has appointed a legal guardian [11][12].

Related timing requirements

1. At least one year before the 18th birthday: The district must give the student and parents written notice of the transfer of rights, plus information and resources on guardianship, alternatives to guardianship (including a supported decision-making agreement under Chapter 1357, Estates Code), and other supports. The IEP must include a statement that this was provided [1][9]. The IEP must also state that the student has been informed of the rights that will transfer at 18 [9][12].

2. When the student reaches 18: The district must give separate written notice to the adult student and the parent that parental rights have transferred. This notice is distinct from the IEP statement and must include the guidance and resources information and contact information for obtaining more information [9][10]. It is not required to contain the elements of notice in 34 CFR §300.503 [10].

3. Effect of notices to the parent: A notice given to the parent after transfer does not create a right for the parent to consent to or participate in the matter. For example, a notice of an admission, review, and dismissal (ARD) committee meeting does not create a right to attend. The adult student or the district may invite individuals with knowledge or special expertise, including the parent, under 34 CFR §300.321(a)(6) [10].

  • Special education (IDEA): Decision-making rights transfer to the student at 18 unless a court has appointed a guardian or the student has set up another arrangement, such as supported decision-making. Texas schools must notify the student and parents of the transfer at least a year in advance, around age 17.
  • Enrollment and absences: An 18-year-old can enroll themselves and typically handle their own paperwork, including sign-offs for absences, though districts set their own procedures.
  • Compulsory attendance: Texas requires attendance from age 6 until age 19, so an 18-year-old is still legally required to attend. Students can be disciplined or face truancy-related consequences, and school districts' policies on adult students' attendance vary.

There are also a few exceptions. A minor who is married, or who has been legally declared an adult through a court order of emancipation, may also be treated as an adult for school purposes before turning 18.
