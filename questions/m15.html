In Texas schools, What is reviewed during a manifestation determination?

Answer key

In a manifestation determination, the ARD committee must review all relevant information in the student's file, including the IEP, any teacher observations, and any relevant information provided by the parent, to decide whether the conduct was caused by, or had a direct and substantial relationship to, the child's disability, or whether it was the direct result of the school's failure to implement the IEP. If either is true, the conduct is a manifestation of the disability. The review must take place within 10 school days of the decision to change the student's placement.

  • The review must occur within 10 school days of any decision to change the placement of a child with a disability because of a violation of a code of student conduct [1].

1. Whether the conduct in question was caused by, or had a direct and substantial relationship to, the child's disability [1][2]; or

If either condition is met, the conduct must be determined to be a manifestation of the child's disability [1][2]. If the conduct was the direct result of a failure to implement the IEP, the LEA must take immediate steps to remedy those deficiencies [1][2].

Related change-of-placement context

A manifestation determination review (MDR) is the process a school uses to decide whether a student with a disability's misconduct was caused by, or directly related to, that disability. It's required under the federal Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA), and Texas follows it through the Texas Education Agency's rules and the Texas Education Code's discipline provisions.

When it happens

An MDR is triggered when a school is considering a disciplinary removal that amounts to a "change of placement," generally a removal of more than 10 consecutive school days, or a pattern of shorter removals that adds up to a change of placement. The review must be held within 10 school days of the decision to change the placement. In Texas, that includes decisions to send a student to a Disciplinary Alternative Education Program (DAEP) or to expel them.

Who reviews it

The review is conducted by the local education agency (the district), the parent, and relevant members of the student's Admission, Review, and Dismissal (ARD) committee. The parent and district decide together who the relevant members are.

What is reviewed

The group looks at all relevant information in the student's file, including:

  • The student's Individualized Education Program (IEP)
  • Teacher observations
  • Any relevant information provided by the parent
  • Evaluations and diagnostic results, and other records about the student's disability and needs
  • Behavior intervention plans, if any

The two questions the team must answer

1. Was the conduct caused by, or did it have a direct and substantial relationship to, the student's disability?

2. Was the conduct the direct result of the district's failure to implement the student's IEP?

What happens next

  • If the answer to either question is yes, the conduct is a manifestation of the disability.
