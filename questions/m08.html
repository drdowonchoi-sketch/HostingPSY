In Texas schools, What happens when a child turns three?

Answer key

Children under age three with developmental delays are served by the Early Childhood Intervention (ECI) program through the Texas Health and Human Services Commission. At age three, a child with a disability may become eligible for special education and related services from the school district, which must ensure FAPE is available by the child's third birthday. Because not every child in ECI qualifies, a transition meeting is held at least 90 calendar days before the toddler turns three; if the child qualifies, special education and related services must be available on the third birthday.

When a child turns three in Texas, the child may become eligible for special education and related services, and the school district becomes responsible for FAPE.

Transition from ECI to special education

  • ECI services, provided through the Texas Health and Human Services Commission, are for children under age three [5].
  • At age three, children with disabilities may become eligible for special education and related services. If so, the child's school district must ensure that FAPE is made available by the child's third birthday [5].
  • At least 90 calendar days before a toddler receiving ECI services turns three, a meeting is scheduled to help the family transition from ECI to special education and related services, if appropriate [5].
  • Not all children who receive ECI services qualify for public school services. If the child qualifies, special education and related services must be made available on the child's third birthday [5].

Eligibility and IEP

  • In Texas, children ages 3 through 21 may be eligible for special education under one of 13 disability categories. The ARD committee makes the eligibility determination and decides the eligibility category, based on an individualized evaluation (the Full Individual and Initial Evaluation) [8].
  • An IEP is the written document completed by the ARD committee for every public-school child ages 3–21 receiving special education services in Texas [6][7].
  • For a child aged three through five, the IEP Team must consider an IFSP that contains the IFSP content described in section 636(d) of the Act and its implementing regulations. The IFSP may serve as the IEP if this is consistent with State policy and agreed to by the agency and the child's parents [1].
  • If the parents choose an IFSP, the public agency must give them a detailed explanation of the differences between an IFSP and an IEP and obtain written informed consent [1].
  • A meeting to develop an IEP must be conducted within 30 days of a determination that the child needs special education and related services. As soon as possible after the IEP is developed, services must be made available in accordance with the IEP [1][3].

Dual enrollment and private school options (ages 3–4)

  • Parents of an eligible student ages 3 or 4 may "dual enroll" the student in both public and private school, beginning on the third birthday. Dual enrollment continues until the end of the school year in which the student turns five, or until the student is eligible for the district's public school kindergarten program, whichever comes first. The district where the student resides is responsible for providing special education and related services to a dually enrolled student [4].
  • Parents of an eligible 3- or 4-year-old who decline dual enrollment may request a services plan as described in 34 CFR §§300.130-300.144. The district where the private school is located is responsible for developing the services plan if the student is designated to receive services under 34 CFR §300.132 [4].

Texas School for the Deaf

  • A parent or guardian may refer a student to the Texas School for the Deaf through the ARD or IFSP committee as an initial referral to special education for students age three or younger [2].

Children who are deaf or hard of hearing or have a visual impairment

  • Children from birth until age three who are eligible as deaf or hard of hearing or as having a visual impairment are entitled to both ECI and public school special education services. The school's services supplement and coordinate with ECI services [5].

At age three, responsibility for a child with a disability shifts from the state's early intervention program to the local school district.

What changesWhen a child receiving ECI services turns three, that child ages out and must exit ECI services. All children must leave ECI by their third birthday. ECI (Early Childhood Intervention, IDEA Part C) is run by Texas HHSC and serves children and their families from birth up to age 3.

From age three, the school district takes over under IDEA Part B. TEA is the lead agency for Part B, which requires a free appropriate public education (FAPE) for all children with disabilities ages 3 through 21; Early Childhood Special Education (ECSE) covers children ages 3 through 5, prior to kindergarten. These services are provided through local school districts and charter schools.

The transition timeline

Around 27–33 months, the transition meeting is scheduled, the IFSP is updated to reflect transition planning, and the ECI provider sends information to the local education agency (LEA).

The ECI provider notifies the LEA at least 90 calendar days before the third birthday that the child is potentially eligible for Part B services — but ECI must get written parental consent first, and if you decline, the process stops.

A transition conference must be held at least 90 days but not more than nine months before the child turns three.

The LEA must notify the parent and obtain signed consent before evaluating a child. A Full and Individual Initial Evaluation (FIIE) is then conducted.

By the third birthday, the ARD (admission, review, and dismissal) committee determines eligibility; if eligible, it decides what services are needed and develops the IEP.

The entire transition process must be completed by the child's third birthday, including evaluation, eligibility determination, and implementation of the IEP.

Special cases

Summer birthdays: the ARD committee determines the IEP start date; services begin on the first day of school, or earlier if stated in the IEP.

Late referrals: if a child is referred to ECI fewer than 45 days before the third birthday and may be eligible for Part B, ECI must, with parental consent, refer the child directly to the LEA for an eligibility determination.

For children with fewer than 90 days of ECI services, the LEA follows standard Part B timelines beginning from the date of parental consent.

Sensory impairments: children who are deaf/hard of hearing or have a visual impairment are entitled to FAPE from birth, so birth-to-36-month services must be coordinated between ECI and the LEA.

Not in ECI, or you changed your mind: contact the Child Find representative at your local school district or the ECSE representative at your Regional Education Service Center. After leaving ECI, you can contact the school district at any time to request an evaluation.
